ARTIST BIO

myer roze (they/them) is a queer & trans memory worker, interdisciplinary artist, oral historian, and community gardener based in Brooklyn. Primarily working in mixed-media sculpture, they draw from archival images, found objects, alternative photographic processes, and textiles to unravel the corporeal limits and speculative possibilities of the trans(sexual) body.

myer's artistic practice emerges from their study of '90s/'00s digital ephemera and their fascination with the surgically re/constructed figure. Engaging Saidiya Hartman's concept of “critical fabulation” alongside Susan Striker's transsexual monster, their artistic inquiry traces themes of disfigurement, embodied (mis)memory, and haunting. Their manipulation of born-digital archival material blurs tensions across the innate/engineered and the memorialized/obscured, offering a world for our ghosts—and ourselves—to refuse.

myer is also a trained oral historian through the Oral History Masters of Arts program at Columbia University.
PROFESSIONAL BIO

Myer Rosenblum (they/them) is a second year Master’s student in Oral History at Columbia University. Their research interests center on transgender movement building in the late-twentieth century, trans internet communities of the ‘90s and ‘00s, and critical archival practices for LGBTQ+ materials.

With roots in community organizing and nonprofits, Myer brings 10+ years of professional experience across LGBTQ+ crisis counseling and suicide prevention, abortion access case management, abolitionist arts education, and neighborhood-based food sovereignty.

Currently, Myer’s ongoing projects include a digital archive of trans personal homepages from the early internet and a collaborative oral history of the Bergen Street Community Garden. They hold a B.A. in American Studies and Gender & Sexuality Studies from the University of Richmond and live in Crown Heights, Brooklyn.
